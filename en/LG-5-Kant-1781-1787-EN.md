# LG-5 — Kant, *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781/1787)

> **Role in the protocol:** the **transcendental method** anchor — asking for the *conditions of possibility* of experience before any proposition about objects. Exactly the protocol's stance: before "what is the world", ask "what must the frame of meaningfulness be" — the very question that makes §3 (silence) and §5 (the revere realm) possible. [[Aligned-Protocol-EN]]

## Exact reference [verified 2026-09-25]

- **Kant, I. (1781). *Critik der reinen Vernunft*. Riga: Johann Friedrich Hartknoch.** (first edition, A)
- **Second edition (B): 1787, same publisher** — a substantial rewrite; both are standard citation targets (dual A/B pagination).
- Publisher and dates verified against rare-book catalogues (Christie's, Whitmore, Bauman: "Riga: Johann Friedrich Hartknoch, 1781" and "Riga: Hartknoch, 1787 — revised and expanded by Kant").
- Key original vocabulary: *Bedingungen der Möglichkeit* (conditions of possibility); *Erscheinung* (appearance) vs. *Ding an sich* (thing in itself); *transzendental* (concerning our mode of knowing objects, not the objects).

## The book's core claim

Questions about the thing-in-itself are unanswerable within the frame of possible experience; cognition arises from the union of intuition and concept, and **the conditions of possibility of experience are simultaneously the conditions of possibility of its objects**. Concepts applied beyond the boundary of possible experience (soul, world-as-a-whole, God) fall into **Antinomies**: reason, by crossing its own boundary, contradicts itself — structurally, not accidentally.

## Links to other anchors

- **Tarski** [[LG-4-Tarski-1955-EN]]: formalizes Kant's Antinomies via the language/metalanguage split — the same "boundary as consistency condition" structure.
- **Russell** [[LG-6-Russell-1908-EN]]: type theory captures the Antinomies in pure logic — a hierarchy of frames prevents self-defeat (membership vs. collection, not appearance/thing-in-itself).
- **Lakatos** [[LG-3-Lakatos-1976-EN]]: Kant thought the transcendental frame immutable; Lakatos showed frames themselves shift in scientific progress — the corpus operationalized this as the register's *registered-event migration*.
